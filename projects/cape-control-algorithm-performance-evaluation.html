CAPE: Control Algorithm Performance Evaluation under Learned Vehicle Dynamics Models

CAPE is a systematic closed-loop benchmarking framework for studying how learned vehicle dynamics models affect autonomous racing controller performance. The framework evaluates heterogeneous controllers under identical settings while injecting realistic uncertainty into sensing, actuation, vehicle parameters, and process dynamics.

Benchmarking Architecture

CAPE evaluates five controllers, PID, RISE, Nominal NMPC, Robust NMPC, and Adaptive NMPC, across three learned vehicle dynamics models: the Deep Pacejka Model (DPM), Deep Dynamics Model (DDM), and the proposed Enhanced Physics Model (EPM). EPM was developed as part of our Physics-Integrated Neural Networks for Autonomous Navigation project and is used here to study how improved learned dynamics translate into controller robustness and lap performance.

CAPE closed-loop benchmarking architecture with controller, learned dynamics, disturbance, sensor uncertainty, measured state feedback, and parametric uncertainty blocks.

The uncertainty pipeline introduces model mismatch through vehicle mass, tire stiffness, and friction variations, while actuator delay, process disturbances, and crosswind perturbations stress the closed-loop response. Sensor uncertainty is modeled through additive Gaussian measurement noise and IMU bias drift, allowing the same controller-model pair to be evaluated from clean operation through progressively noisier conditions.

Clean Closed-Loop Evaluation

In the clean benchmark, all controller-model pairs are evaluated with identical controller settings and constraints, without noise or uncertainty. The trajectory, longitudinal tracking error, and lateral tracking error reveal how model fidelity influences controller behavior even before disturbances are introduced.

Clean closed-loop trajectory tracking for DPM, DDM, and EPM across PID, RISE, Nominal NMPC, Robust NMPC, and Adaptive NMPC.

Closed-loop velocity, yaw-rate, throttle, and steering responses for each controller across DPM, DDM, and EPM.

Under clean conditions, EPM-based controller rollouts follow the raceline more closely and maintain smaller longitudinal and lateral error magnitudes. The response plots show how this improved tracking appears across both vehicle states and control commands.

Uncertainty and Noise Robustness

CAPE sweeps a global noise factor from η = 0 to η = 5 in increments of 0.5. This sweep progressively increases sensing, actuation, process, and parametric uncertainties, exposing which controller-model combinations remain stable as model mismatch grows. Failed laps are handled with a bounded tracking-error penalty and a success-rate penalized lap-completion time, so unstable controller-model combinations are reflected in the aggregate results.

Aggregate mean longitudinal and lateral tracking errors, plus success-rate penalized lap completion times, across model-controller combinations.

5.82 s EPM with Adaptive NMPC penalized lap time.
8.80 s DDM with Adaptive NMPC penalized lap time.
12.99 s DPM with Adaptive NMPC penalized lap time.

EPM achieves the lowest aggregate longitudinal and lateral tracking errors across the uncertainty sweep. Its advantage is most pronounced for NMPC-based controllers, where model prediction accuracy directly shapes the planned trajectory and control inputs. PID and RISE show less separation between learned models because they do not explicitly optimize over a prediction model, but the EPM pairing still improves robustness in the broader benchmark. At η = 1, EPM reduces average longitudinal tracking error by 29.0 percent and 17.2 percent, and lateral tracking error by 24.6 percent and 12.3 percent relative to DPM and DDM, respectively.

Noisy closed-loop trajectory tracking with global noise factor η = 1.

Temporal longitudinal and lateral tracking error evolution across all controllers and learned dynamics models during the uncertainty sweep.

As uncertainty increases, DPM and DDM accumulate larger transient deviations, oscillatory tracking behavior, and more failed runs. Across all evaluations, DPM fails 31 times, DDM fails 27 times, and EPM fails 12 times. For NMPC-based controllers only, EPM completes all 33 evaluations with zero failures, while DPM and DDM fail 19 and 12 times respectively.

MPC Model Ablation

The MPC ablation study separates the effect of the internal model used by the controller optimizer from the learned dynamics model used for closed-loop propagation. Solid trajectories keep the internal controller model fixed as EPM while varying the learned dynamics model, while dashed trajectories vary the internal model while keeping EPM as the learned dynamics model.

MPC ablation study showing raceline adherence, lap completion, and track violations for matched and mismatched internal-controller and learned-dynamics model combinations.

The matched EPM controller and EPM learned dynamics configuration produces the closest raceline adherence and the fastest successful lap completion. Mismatched model combinations lead to larger tracking deviations and track-boundary violations, especially in high-curvature sections, highlighting the importance of model fidelity inside both controller planning and closed-loop propagation.
